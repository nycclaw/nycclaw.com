The system is powerful. But the operational overhead of running it falls on your team.

Case phases need constant manual updates

A demand letter goes out. Treatment wraps up. A settlement offer comes in. Each of these should trigger a phase change in Filevine. Instead, they sit until someone remembers to update the project. By then, the team is working off stale information. Phase tracking only works when someone is actually tracking phases.

The team doesn't know what happened since yesterday

An attorney picks up a case file Monday morning. What happened over the weekend? Did the adjuster respond? Was the client's deposition rescheduled? Did new medical records come in? They have to dig through notes, emails, and activity logs to piece it together. That's 15 minutes per case before any real work starts.

Correspondence tracking falls behind

Emails come in from adjusters, opposing counsel, medical providers, and clients. Some get logged to the right Filevine project. Some get forwarded to a paralegal who logs them later. Some sit in someone's inbox for three days. The case file is supposed to be the single source of truth, but it's always a step behind reality.

PI and mass tort intake is high volume and repetitive

Your firm gets dozens of intake calls and web submissions a week. Each one needs the same qualifying questions: what type of accident, when it happened, medical treatment status, prior representation. Your intake team spends hours on calls that don't qualify. The ones that do qualify sometimes wait too long for a callback.

OpenClaw + Filevine Integration

OpenClaw connects to Filevine through the API. It reads your projects, updates fields, logs activity, and handles the operational work your team keeps putting off.

Automatic phase updates

OpenClaw monitors project activity and advances phases based on what's actually happening. Demand letter sent? Phase moves to "Demand." Settlement offer received? Phase moves to "Negotiation." No one has to remember to drag anything. The case file reflects reality in real time.

Intake qualification that runs itself

A potential client fills out your intake form or calls your answering service. OpenClaw picks it up, asks qualifying questions (accident type, date, treatment status, prior counsel), and creates the Filevine project with all custom fields populated if they qualify. Unqualified leads get a polite decline. Your intake team handles fewer calls, better cases.

Correspondence logged to the right project

OpenClaw reads incoming emails and messages, identifies which case they belong to, and logs them as notes on the correct Filevine project. Adjuster responses, client updates, medical provider communications. Everything lands where it should, tagged and timestamped, without anyone copying and pasting.

Daily team briefings per case

Every morning (or whenever you want), OpenClaw compiles what changed on each active case since the last briefing. New documents uploaded, notes added, phase changes, upcoming deadlines. Your attorneys get a concise summary instead of spending 15 minutes per case catching up.

Deadline monitoring and alerts

OpenClaw watches your Filevine calendar events and task due dates. Statute of limitations approaching? Discovery deadline in 10 days? Client follow-up overdue by a week? It flags these to the responsible team member before they become problems. Not a generic reminder. A specific alert with case context.

Custom field updates from documents

When new documents land in a Filevine project, OpenClaw can read them and pull relevant data into your custom fields. Medical bill amounts, treatment dates, insurance policy limits. The information that paralegals spend hours extracting gets populated automatically, ready for review.

What is OpenClaw?

OpenClaw is an open-source AI assistant that connects to the tools you already use and operates them for you. Email, calendar, CRM, case management. It reads, it responds, it takes action. It runs on your own devices, so your data stays yours.

It's not a chatbot you paste into a website. It's an operator that works behind the scenes, handling the tasks you keep putting off because there aren't enough hours in the day.

Common questions

Does OpenClaw replace Filevine?
No. OpenClaw connects to Filevine through the API and works inside it. Your projects, documents, custom fields, and team permissions stay exactly where they are. OpenClaw handles the repetitive operational work so your team can focus on practicing law.
What Filevine features can OpenClaw access?
OpenClaw connects to Filevine's API for projects, contacts, tasks, documents, notes, custom fields, calendar events, and phase tracking. If Filevine exposes it through the API, OpenClaw can read it, update it, and act on it.
How long does the integration setup take?
Most Filevine integrations are running within 3 to 5 days. The API connection goes live on day one. The remaining time is spent configuring OpenClaw for your specific case types, phase structures, and team workflows. Within a week, it's handling routine updates on its own.
Does this work with our custom fields and phase templates?
Yes. Filevine's API exposes custom field sections and phase structures. We configure OpenClaw to understand your specific setup, including your custom fields for case values, injury types, insurance info, or whatever your firm tracks. It reads and writes to the same fields your team uses.
Can OpenClaw handle intake for high-volume practice areas like PI or mass tort?
That's one of the strongest use cases. OpenClaw responds to intake inquiries, asks qualifying questions based on your criteria (accident type, statute of limitations, medical treatment status), and creates the Filevine project with all relevant fields populated if the lead qualifies. It handles the volume so your intake team focuses on the cases that actually need a human conversation.
